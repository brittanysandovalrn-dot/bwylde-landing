Where This Came From +−

You were shaped by hands that weren't yours, assigned roles before anybody asked whether you wanted them, handed a whole life that fit the people around you better than it ever fit you. And you learned it. You got good in there.

Feral is what comes after that.

Not the animal kind. This is feral on human terms only: molded, shaped, assigned a life, taught the rules, got good in there, chose to go anyway. That's the whole definition, and it's why the word fit better than anything softer would have.

What's on the other side isn't a better performance. It's inhabiting the thing instead of performing in it. Fulfillment doesn't come from a new job, a new city, a new body, a new relationship. It comes from alignment: days finally matching the person living them, work finally pointing at actual purpose instead of somebody else's idea of it.

Britt didn't build FERAL from theory. They ran every letter of it on their own life first, because proof does more than promise. They're thirty-four, nonbinary, and raising four kids alone while building this out loud. There's no phase of Feel, Examine, Release, Align, or Live they haven't actually lived, usually with a kid in the next room and zero time to make it look graceful.

The same five moves showed up first in Britt's own recovery, then in solo parenting, then in rebuilding after a relationship ended, then in leading, then in stepping into their own name. Same framework, different room every time. Not a fix for one problem. The thing underneath all of them.

FERAL: The Framework +−
The Framework

FERAL

The way back to the wild you were born to live from. Not a ladder, a spiral: same five moves, run backward, sideways, and forward.

Backward

On a choice already made and never understood.

Sideways

On the decision sitting in front of you right now.

Forward

On the ones not yet made.

F
Feel
Raw, unmanaged

Years in a society that runs on emotional suppression mean feeling often starts as relearning how to connect to your body at all. Stop handling the feeling and let it be there first, no narrating, no fixing, no explaining it away to whoever's watching, so there's something real underneath to examine.

numb→present
E
Examine
Gentle, no judgment

Examination is validation, acceptance of what surfaced, letting it exist instead of arguing it back down. What's never acknowledged can't be released, and you can't release what you never let yourself see. Soft on purpose. If this phase had teeth you'd white-knuckle past it into Release and nothing would come loose.

shame→acceptance
R
Release
Freeing, a little grief

Set down what was never yours to carry. The role, the expectation, the version somebody else needed. Comes with grief, because you're also letting go of who you were while you carried it.

carrying→unburdened
A
Align
Clarifying, decisive

Root back into what's actually true about your safety and your own self-trust right now, not the old story that says you're still unsafe. Stops being internal and starts costing something, and some things don't survive the change. That's the process working, not failing.

drifting→directed
L
Live
Steady, self-directed

Where alignment stops being internal and becomes action: do the next right thing, then do it again when it's boring and nobody noticed. Nobody is coming to validate this. That isn't the bad news, that's the entire point.

knowing→inhabiting

Nobody graduates. FERAL is the process you run again every time you drift, and it walks you back.

Workshop

FERAL for Teams

The same five moves, reframed for a room instead of one person.

FFeel. Name what's actually happening instead of performing fine.
EExamine. Conflict becomes mutual understanding, not blame.
RRelease. Let go of the roles and turf in the way of the work.
AAlign. Match strengths to a shared purpose.
LLive. Do it on a regular Tuesday, not just at the offsite.

Teams leave more creative, more willing to bring their whole selves to work, and actually engaged instead of checked out.

Teams don't follow the title, they follow the nervous system running it. The same five moves that walk a person back to themselves build mutual understanding on a team too, since most conflict is a few people bracing against something nobody's said out loud. What's left is a leader with the confidence, clarity, and direction to actually run the room, and a workplace that shifts because of it.
